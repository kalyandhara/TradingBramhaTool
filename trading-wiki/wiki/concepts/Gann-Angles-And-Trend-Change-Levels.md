# Gann Angles & the Trend Change Level (TCL)

Documented because a daily letter this desk now reads
([[sources/Bramesh-NIFTY-Gann-1x3-Higher-High-2026-09-18]]) uses this vocabulary as its
primary frame. **Nothing here is validated.** The page exists so the terms can be read,
not so they can be relied on.

## Definition

**Gann angles** are trendlines drawn at fixed **price-per-unit-of-time** slopes from a
significant pivot, rather than fitted to the data. The family is named by the ratio of
time units to price units:

| Angle | Slope | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| 1×8, 1×4, 1×3, 1×2 | shallow → steep | **support** in an uptrend; a rising market *above* these is healthy |
| **1×1** ("45°") | 1 price unit per 1 time unit | Gann's centre line — the balance between price and time |
| 2×1, 3×1, 4×1, 8×1 | steep | unsustainable advance; typically resistance in a downtrend |

The governing idea is that price and time are in balance on the 1×1, and that **breaking
one angle means falling to the next** — the angles form a ladder, not isolated lines.

A **1×3 angle** is therefore shallow: three time units per one price unit. Rallying off it
is the weakest-slope version of "still in structure" — which is precisely why the letter's
framing of a bounce there as *foundational* should be read as **the last line before the
structure fails**, not as strength.

### The scale problem, which is the whole problem

A Gann angle requires a **price unit per bar** before it can be drawn at all. "45°" is
meaningless on its own — it depends entirely on the chart's aspect ratio and the chosen
scaling. Two analysts with the same pivot and different units get different levels and both
call them 1×1. **Any Gann level cited without its scaling is not reproducible.** The source
letter does not state its scaling.

**Related, also undefined in the source:** *"double ingress"* and *"Gann harmonic number"*
were used without construction. They are recorded as terms encountered, not as methods.

## Trend Change Level (TCL)

A **single price that flips the operative bias** — above it the stance is long, below it the
stance is short or flat — published in two horizons:

* **Positional TCL** — the swing/positional flip (2026-09-18: 23,372 on NIFTY)
* **Intraday TCL** — the session's flip (2026-09-18: 23,327)

**What is genuinely useful here is the discipline, not the arithmetic.** A TCL is a
pre-committed, single, falsifiable number that decides direction *before* the session — and
this desk already values exactly that (see the pre-registration practice in
[[analyses/Open-Backlog]]). A letter that prints one number and lives with it is more
checkable than one that narrates.

**What is not useful:** the construction is undisclosed, so a TCL cannot be computed here,
only copied. A level you cannot derive is a level you cannot backtest.

> [!warning] Spot between the TCL and the invalidation is NOT a signal
> On 2026-09-18 NIFTY sat at 23,270, below the 23,372 TCL and above the 23,135
> invalidation. The letter was labelled **bullish**, but by its own rules the bull
> condition had not triggered. **A stated bias and a triggered level are different
> things**, and the gap between them is where a reader takes a trade the author did not.

## Common mistakes

1. **Citing an angle without its price-per-bar scaling** — the level is then unreproducible.
2. **Reading a bounce off a shallow angle (1×3, 1×4) as strength.** It is the opposite: the
   steeper angles have already been lost.
3. **Treating the TCL as a target.** It is a bias switch; it says nothing about how far.
4. **Back-drawing from a pivot chosen after the fact.** Angles anchored to a hindsight
   pivot always look predictive.
