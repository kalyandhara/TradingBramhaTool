# Sakata's Method

## Definition
Sakata's Method (also: Sakata's Law, Honma Constitution, Sakata's Five Methods) is the foundational philosophical and pattern framework underlying all Japanese candlestick analysis. Developed by **Munehisa Honma** in 18th-century Japan, it distills market psychology into five core formations. All continuation candle patterns trace back to these five formations.

---

## Historical Origin

- **Munehisa Honma** (also Sohkyu Honma): Born to a wealthy rice-trading family in **Sakata**, Dewa Province (modern Yamagata Prefecture), western Honshu
- Took control of the family business in **1750**, began trading at the Sakata rice exchange
- Built a private communications network: men on rooftops every 4 km from **Sakata to Osaka** (~380 miles, 100+ men) → information edge before any telegraph
- Studied investor psychology exhaustively; kept detailed records to understand crowd behavior
- **Honma's rule:** If you feel compelled to rush a trade because you "can't lose," wait 3 days. If you still feel the same, enter — successfully.
- Died **1803**. His 160 rules (written at age 51) became the basis of candle pattern philosophy
- **1755:** "San-en Kinsen Horoku" published — "If all other people are bullish, be foolish and sell rice" — Japan's original contrarian manifesto
- Today his former home in Sakata is the **Honma Museum of Art**

## The Five Core Formations (Go = Five in Japanese)

Sakata's Method deals with 5 basic market formations using the number **San (Three)** as the organizing principle:

| Formation | Japanese Name | Meaning |
|-----------|---------------|---------|
| **Three Mountains** | San-zan | Triple top reversal; market peaks three times; classic top pattern |
| **Three Rivers** | San-sen | Triple bottom reversal; market finds support three times; classic bottom pattern |
| **Three Gaps** | San-ku | Three consecutive gaps in one direction; trend exhaustion signal |
| **Three Methods** | San-poh | Continuation pattern: thrust → brief consolidation (3 small opposite bars) → resumption |
| **Three Soldiers** | San-pei | Three consecutive long bars in one direction; strong trend confirmation |

---

## Three Methods (San-poh) — The Most Actionable Formation

The most tradeable formation from Sakata's five. Modern expression = **Rising/Falling Three Methods** (4–5 bar continuation pattern):

**Rising Three Methods:**
1. Long white body (initial thrust)
2–4. Three small black bodies, all contained within range of bar 1 (consolidation/rest)
5. Long white body that closes above bar 1's close (resumption)

**Psychology:** The market pauses to gather breath, weak holders exit, then strong hands resume direction. The **consolidation is the trade setup** — buy the break above bar 1's close.

**Mat Hold:** Variant where a gap is preserved through the consolidation; even more bullish as the gap acts as support.

---

## Honma's Market Psychology Insights (From 160 Rules)

1. Do not rush entry; wait 3 days if you feel certain
2. Study the psychology of others — their fear and greed create your opportunity
3. Wealth from trading comes from understanding sentiment cycles, not just patterns
4. Records + systematic observation are the true edge (Honma's rooftop network = information + recording)
5. Contrarian stance: when everyone is bullish, sell; when everyone is bearish, buy

---

## Common Mistakes
- Treating Sakata's patterns as isolated signals without Honma's core lesson: **psychology precedes pattern**
- The Three Methods setup fails when the consolidation bars violate the range of bar 1 — that signals trend failure, not continuation
