# Intrinsic Value and Valuation Context

## Definition

**Intrinsic value** is what a business is worth from the cash or earnings it can be expected to produce, independent of its quoted price. **Valuation** is the comparison of price to that estimate. A stock can be a good business and a bad purchase at the same time.

**Multiple compression** is price falling while earnings do not: the P/E (price ÷ earnings) shrinks. It is the mechanism the Carnevale video blames for its "fallen angels".

## How to Use

Three different yardsticks, which answer three different questions and must not be mixed:

| Yardstick | Question | Needs |
|---|---|---|
| Versus the **market** today | Is this expensive compared with other stocks right now? | A cross-section (the desk has this: `fundamental_score`) |
| Versus its **own history** | Is this expensive for *this* company? | Many years of EPS and price per stock (the desk has **4**) |
| Versus an **intrinsic-value estimate** | What is it worth? | A model: earnings × a justified multiple, or discounted cash flow |

## Variants / Parameters

P/E, P/B, P/S, PEG, dividend yield, earnings yield; EPS-times-normal-multiple (the Fast Graphs construct); DCF. Each hides a choice: which earnings, which "normal" multiple, which growth.

## Common Mistakes

- Reading a price chart as evidence of value.
- Calibrating "cheap" and "expensive" on another market. NSE is structurally dearer than the US: the desk's own calibration found median P/E 35.7x and p90 92.5x, so US bands would mark almost everything overvalued.
- Treating a low P/E as safe: it can be a value trap, with earnings about to fall.
- Judging a rule from a hand-picked list of winners or losers with no base rate.

## Status on this desk

- Valuation exists as one scored axis (P/E, P/B, PEG) relative to the NSE cross-section, **excluded from the business-profile rank on purpose** and **not used in any shortlist, vote or verdict**.
