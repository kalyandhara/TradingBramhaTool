# Kalyan's Trading Philosophy & Rules

> Distilled from personal notes in trading_learning_raw.pdf

## Core Mental Model
The market is like Animal Planet. Operators (big fish) want to trap retail investors (small fish). Reaction, rush, greed, and aggressiveness make retail vulnerable to big losses.

**The antidote: Don't react. Respond.**

## The 4 Commandments

### 1. Keep Cash During Blood Bath
Correct-time entry only possible when you have cash available during market dips.
→ Always keep reserve. Never be fully invested.

### 2. Follow Context-Appropriate Strategy
- **In uptrend:** DarvaX / TechnoFunda analysis
- **In downtrend:** Look for quality large caps with convincing bright future
- **When downtrend sensed (RSI/EMA signals):** Book maximum profit possible
  - Don't worry about brokerage on blue chips — you can re-enter any time

### 3. Market Always Has Opportunity
At any point in time, there are stocks offering at least 10% upside.
Never FOMO — new opportunities always emerge.

### 4. Market Prices the Future, Not the Past
"Market don't care about past, present — it only cares about future."
Estimate future EPS and future PE to estimate price.

## Risk / Position Sizing Rules
- **SME exposure:** < 20% of total portfolio
- **Single stock exposure:** < 10% of total portfolio
- **Invest in your circle of competence**

## Real Portfolio Lesson (Documented)
- Jan 2, 2025: Portfolio +₹2 lakh (4% up)
- Jan 13, 2025: Portfolio -₹4 lakh (−10% down)
- Net swing: −15% in 11 days
- **Lesson:** When RSI/EMA signals downtrend, book profits aggressively. Don't wait.

## Valuation Warning
Low Growth + High Valuations = Wealth Destruction
Example: EPS ₹1, PE 100 → Price ₹100. After 1 year with 20% EPS growth but PE contracts to 50: Price = ₹60 (−40% despite EPS growth).
→ **Never ignore PE contraction risk in high-valuation stocks.**

## Cyclical Stocks Note
Some stocks have seasonal patterns (e.g., Monte Carlo Fashions: low Mar/May, high Aug/Dec).
Thomas Scott: low Mar/Aug, high Aug/Dec.
→ Track seasonal patterns for better entry timing.

## Minervini Alignment & Reinforcements (2026-06-15)

From [Think and Trade Like a Champion](../sources/Minervini-Think-Trade-Champion.md):

**Strong reinforcements of existing philosophy:**
- **Commandment 1 (Keep Cash)** = Minervini's "scale up on wins, scale down on losses; pilot buy first" — both emphasize not being fully deployed until the market proves itself
- **Commandment 2 (context-appropriate strategy)** = Minervini's Stage Analysis — uptrend = SEPA/VCP; downtrend = cash or shorts
- **Commandment 3 (Market always has opportunity)** = Minervini's "don't force trades; sit-out power; wait for setups"
- **Risk / Position Sizing** (single stock <10%): Minervini suggests 20–25% as *optimal* for concentrated growth portfolio; this is more conservative — appropriate for India/swing hybrid

**New rules to add from Minervini:**
1. **Hard stop-loss, never mental** — "those who trade without stops eventually stop trading"; max 10%, avg target 5–6%
2. **50/80 Rule** — once a market leader tops, 80% chance of ≥50% decline; 50% chance of ≥80% decline → NEVER average down into a breaking leader
3. **Track the Trading Triangle** — know your avg gain, avg loss, and batting average at all times; base risk-sizing on actual results (RBA), not theoretical targets
4. **Base count discipline** — count consolidation stages; do not buy a 5th or 6th base; start lightening at base 3–4
5. **Process over outcome** — take a small disciplined loss as a win; associate pain with large uncontrolled losses, not small rule-based exits

**No contradictions** — existing philosophy is fully consistent with Minervini; this book deepens and formalizes it.

> Note: The Connors no-stop approach ([Connors-MA-Quantified-Approach-2013](../sources/Connors-MA-Quantified-Approach-2013.md)) is strategy-specific (mean-reversion dip-buying) and does NOT apply to growth/breakout trades. Minervini's stop discipline governs all SEPA trades.

## Multi-Trader Insights from Prop Trading Secrets (2026-06-17)

From [Prop-Trading-Secrets-Lien-Crete-2025](../sources/Prop-Trading-Secrets-Lien-Crete-2025.md) — 14 successful traders interviewed:

**Convergent insights that strengthen this philosophy:**

1. **One instrument focus** (consistent with "circle of competence"): All 14 traders master 1–2 instruments. Extending to NSE: master VCP + EMA pullback first before adding any other setup.

2. **Three-strikes rule** (new addition to daily rules): After 3 losing trades in any session → stop. Apply this to NSE intraday/swing entries. Protects against the revenge-trading cascade documented in the Jan 2025 portfolio lesson.

3. **Overfunded + underleveraged** (extends "SME exposure < 20%" rule): When entering any position, ensure the position size feels "too small" relative to account. Ali Crooks: "risk so small it makes you uncomfortable" = correct risk level. This prevents the panic that leads to poor exits.

4. **Process data during drawdowns** (extends "don't react" commandment): When in a drawdown period, go to data (historical system stats, journal review) not to strategy changes. Multiple traders held through 20–30% drawdowns by anchoring to their edge's data.

5. **First trade sets the tone**: Never open a day's trading with a maximum-size position. Pilot buy first (already in Minervini alignment); confirm the setup is working before adding.

6. **Daily + weekly profit goals with protection**: Extend the "keep cash" commandment — set a daily target; once hit, trail stops and stop adding new positions. Cash preservation during strong stretches compounds just as much as it does during crashes.

7. **Track record before seeking external capital**: Aatu Kokkila (2–3 years before AUM), Andres Granger (3+ years before fund) — external capital requires demonstrated consistency first. Relevant if prop firm or investor capital is part of Kalyan's future path.

**New rules to add from this book:**
- **Three-strikes per session**: hard stop after 3 losses, no exceptions
- **First trade size**: start at 25–50% of intended position; add only after confirming setup is working
- **Video trade review**: record screen during trading; review at slow speed to identify error patterns
- **Daily profit goal protection**: set target; once hit, protect not chase

**No contradictions** — all 14 traders independently confirm: hard stops (contra Connors exception already documented), small position sizing, resilience through drawdowns, process over outcome.

## David Hannan (Laptop Legend) Insights — Pyramiding, Pareto, and Mean Reversion (2026-06-18)

From [YT-David-Hannan-Laptop-Legend](../sources/YT-David-Hannan-Laptop-Legend.md) — small cap trader, $2K→$9M, 41% win rate:

**Pareto distribution of trading profits (validated empirically):**
- Hannan's 2024 data: top 14% of trading days = 82% of annual profits
- Implication: size matters more than frequency; the right response is to size UP on A+ setups, not to grind every day equally
- Application for Kalyan: track which setups and which days are driving disproportionate P&L; once identified, those are the setups to practice exponential sizing on (not 8–20x yet, but starting at 2–3x and building from there)

**Exponential bet sizing (new sizing framework to test):**
- Standard setup: 1x base risk
- Good setup (known pattern, good structure): 2–3x
- A+ setup (all checklist factors aligned): start experimenting with 4–5x
- NOT immediate 8–20x — that is Hannan's level after a decade; calibrate progression

**Pyramiding into sweep-reverse scalp:**
- Kalyan's sweep-reverse entry is the initial leg (DBR + spring)
- After the first target is hit and price consolidates, look for a second sweep-and-reclaim at the next level
- Add vs. the low of the second wash (same technique, recycled risk)
- This is exactly the "wash and reclaim" add technique Hannan described for Bitcoin

**Break-even philosophy validated:**
- Hannan's rule: if trade returns to average in big size = thesis already invalidated → exit immediately
- Aligns perfectly with Kalyan's "tap and go" approach: if it hangs around rather than moving immediately, it's not working
- Largest losses in both Kalyan's Jan 2025 experience and Hannan's career came from holding positions that were "not working" hoping for a reversal

**Mean reversion on Nifty/BankNifty:**
- When Nifty/BankNifty has 4–6 consecutive up days with expanding range and gaps → first red day / gap-fill setup has higher win rate than a breakout entry in the same index
- This is a higher-conviction setup type for intraday: the rubber band is stretched, the reversal is inevitable, timing it with intraday entry (first break of low-of-day) provides clean R:R

**"Broken slot machine" — define your edge precisely:**
- Kalyan's edge = sweep-reverse scalp on liquid Indian indices (Nifty/BankNifty/Sensex)
- Do not dilute this by chasing every pattern; the broken slot machine is the sweep-reverse on the session open
- Resist expanding to setups where the edge is less defined just because they look interesting

**No contradictions** with existing philosophy. Hannan reinforces: process over outcome; hard max losses at broker level; cut size quickly when setup not working; Pareto is real — make sure the big bets come on the right setups.

## Samir Varmer Validation of Kalyan's Scalping Approach (2026-06-18)

From [YT-Samir-Varmer-Physicist-Trader](../sources/YT-Samir-Varmer-Physicist-Trader.md) — PhD physicist, 8-figure systematic futures trader, 30 years:

**Direct validation of the sweep-reverse scalp:**

Kalyan described his approach to Varmer live on the podcast: "I see a demand area get swept, I wait for a confirmation (structure break), I enter long, stop at the low of the sweep, target the session high." Varmer responded: "You're the leech on the shark. You're correctly identifying an iceberg whale order and riding alongside it."

**Why the strategy works (Varmer's mechanism):**
- Large institution executes multi-hour buy order via VWAP algo
- VWAP algo has "trader logic" subroutine: knows stops cluster at predictable levels (demand zones, swing lows, round numbers)
- Algo deliberately slows buying near those levels → price drifts into stops → stops trigger → algo accelerates buying at better price → price rebounds
- The sweep + reversal is the VWAP algo completing its cheap-fill phase and resuming execution
- Kalyan's confirmation (structure break upward) is essentially the signal that the algo has resumed buying

**Valid losses explained:**
"Something else looked like the iceberg but wasn't." When a sweep doesn't reverse: the predictable level had less institutional interest than it appeared; the algo wasn't the cause; the stop cluster was genuine selling pressure.

**New tactical improvements from Varmer:**

1. **Never stop at round numbers** — place stop at a non-round level (e.g., not 100.00; use 99.83 or 99.71); round-number stops are the most targeted by algos
2. **Double-tap sweeps are normal** — increasingly, VWAP algos test a level twice; this is NOT invalidation of the setup; widen stop tolerance to include a second sweep of similar magnitude
3. **Protected low is the right stop location** — Kalyan already does this correctly; Varmer confirms it is statistically less obvious to algos than standard support-line stops
4. **Personality-strategy alignment confirmed** — Varmer's two questions: "Can you sleep holding overnight?" and "Can you handle strings of losses?" → Kalyan's answers align with intraday trend-following; the scalp approach is the right match for his personality

**Regime filter (new addition):**
- Varmer's 200-day MA regime: 2/3 of equity returns above the line, 2/3 of risk below it
- Application: on days where the index (Nifty/SPY) is below its 200-day MA, cut intraday scalp size to 50%; the probability of a genuine VWAP-driven sweep-and-reverse is lower (more two-sided/chaotic flow)
- On days where the index is above its 200-day MA: trade at standard size

**System robustness check (Varmer's noise test):**
Apply to Kalyan's scalp setup: take a dataset of historical sweeps and outcomes, add random noise to the entry criteria (vary the confirmation threshold, the sweep depth, the time window), and verify that results degrade smoothly — not in spikes/humps. If they degrade smoothly, the edge is real. If there are local optima, the current parameters are overfit.

**No contradictions** — Varmer reinforces: process over outcome; rules followed before entering a trade; no discretion at trade time ("if the system says do it, I just do it"); valid losses are unavoidable and not bad management.
