# Book — Think and Trade Like a Champion

## Metadata
- **Author**: Mark Minervini
- **Published**: 2017, Access Publishing Group, LLC
- **File**: `C:\Users\dhara\Downloads\Mark Minervini - Think & Trade Like a Champion_...libgen.li.pdf` (82 pages extracted)
- **Companion to**: *Trade Like a Stock Market Wizard* (McGraw-Hill, 2013) — this book covers content that couldn't fit in the first

## Key Takeaways

1. **Always go in with a plan** — entry trigger, risk plan, profit plan, and contingency responses defined before the trade
2. **Risk-first approach** — know your exit before entry; stop-loss is non-negotiable; "those who trade without a stop eventually stop trading"
3. **Never risk more than you expect to gain** — losses are a function of expected gain; use Result-Based Assumptions (RBA), not theoretical ones
4. **Know the truth about your trading** — track avg gain, avg loss, batting average (PWT); use the Trading Triangle; measure everything
5. **Compound money, not mistakes** — never average down; the 50/80 Rule: once a leader tops, 80% chance of 50% decline; 50% chance of 80% decline
6. **Stage 2 only** — buy ONLY stocks in Stage 2 uptrend; Trend Template (8 criteria) confirms Stage 2; >95% of massive winners were in Stage 2
7. **VCP (Volatility Contraction Pattern)** — the entry trigger; 2-6 contractions from left to right, each ~half the prior; pivot buy on volume surge
8. **Position sizing** — 1.25–2.5% equity risk per trade; optimal 20–25% position; 4–12 stocks total; never >50% in one name
9. **Sell rules** — base count (late-stage = danger); climax top (25–50% in 1–3 weeks); P/E expansion ≥2× from base 1; 70%+ up days in 7–15 period = exhaustion
10. **Eight Keys to Superperformance** — 4 for upside (timing, concentration, turnover, risk/reward); 4 for drawdown control (sell into strength, trade small before big, trade directionally, protect breakeven)
11. **Champion mindset** — mental rehearsal (not visualization), box breathing, NLP cognitive conditioning, celebrate small losses taken correctly

## Detailed Notes

### Section 1 — Always Go in with a Plan

- Entry mechanism, risk management, profit-locking, position sizing — all pre-defined
- **Tennis ball vs egg**: after breakout, healthy stocks bounce back within 2–14 days on contracting volume; unhealthy stocks go splat
- **Follow-through count**: want 3 up days out of 4, or 6 of 8 — institutional accumulation takes multiple days
- **Violations soon after breakout** (any one warrants caution, multiple = sell):
  - Low volume out of base / high volume back in
  - Three or more consecutive lower lows without supportive buying
  - More down days than up days; more bad closes than good
  - Close below 20-day MA
  - Close below 50-day MA on heavy volume
  - Full retracement of a good-size gain
- **MVP Indicator** (David Ryan): 12 of 15 days up + volume +25% + price +20% in same 15 days → institutional accumulation signal (NOT a late-stage sell signal — context matters)
- **Squat and Reversal Recovery**: stock stalls at breakout day, then recovers within ~10 days — don't panic, give it time

### Section 2 — Approach Every Trade Risk-First

- Set stop-loss before entry; execute without question when hit
- Mental stops don't work — use hard stops or automated alerts
- Emotional stop >> actual stop = financial and psychological damage
- **Danger point**: buy as close to the danger point as possible (minimizes risk in dollars)
- You control: what to buy, how much, when to buy, and when to sell — nothing else

### Section 3 — Never Risk More Than You Expect to Gain

- **Batting Average (PWT)** = % winning trades; target ≥40–50%
- **Optimal gain/loss ratio** at 40% PWT = 20%/10%; at 50% PWT shifts to ~48%/24%
- Losses work geometrically: 10% loss needs 11.1% gain; 50% loss needs 100% gain
- **Staggered stops**: bracket around target risk (e.g., 3%/5%/8% on thirds) to stay in two-thirds of position
- Raise stop to breakeven when stock rises to 3× your initial risk, especially if above avg gain
- **Add and Reduce**: pyramid on winning trades; let profits finance additional risk; zero new principal at risk on second buy

### Section 4 — Know the Truth About Your Trading

- **Trading Triangle**: avg win / avg loss / batting average — must all be tracked
- **Spreadsheet discipline**: every trade, every result, monthly tracker; largest gain and loss in a month; hold time for gains vs losses
- **Stubborn Trader indicators**: if your largest losers > largest gainers on average, or you hold losses longer than gains → bad pattern
- **Bell curve of trades**: aim for curve skewed right; "The Wall" = max acceptable loss (10%); contain left side, let right side run
- **RBAF** (Result-Based Assumption Forecast): model future performance from your actual avg win/loss/PWT/position size

### Section 5 — Compound Money, Not Mistakes

- Never average down ("Losers average losers" — Paul Tudor Jones)
- **50/80 Rule**: once a secular market leader tops → 80% probability of ≥50% decline; 50% probability of ≥80% decline
- "Cheap" stocks after a decline are usually expensive — avoid the cheap trap
- **Differential disclosure**: if a stock gaps down on seemingly good earnings, institutions know something you don't — get out
- Pilot buy concept: start small, scale up only on evidence of working; scale down on losses
- Scaling sequence: quarter position → half → full, financed by paper profits

### Section 6 & 7 — How and When to Buy

- **Stage 2 only**: >95% of superperformance stocks made gains in Stage 2 uptrend
- **Trend Template (8 criteria)**: see [strategies/Minervini-SEPA.md](../strategies/Minervini-SEPA.md)
- **VCP**: 2–6 successive price contractions, each ~half prior; volume contracts on right side; pivot buy on volume expansion
- **VCP footprint notation**: `WW depth/final T_count` — e.g., `27W 28/6 3T` = 27-week base, 28% to 6% contractions, 3 contractions
- **Overhead supply**: trapped buyers create supply; VCP systematically eliminates supply from weak to strong hands
- **3-C Pattern (Cup Completion Cheat)**: early entry; 4 steps: downtrend → uptrend → pause (cheat, ±5–10%) → breakout above pause high
- **Low Cheat**: entry in lower third of base; riskier but higher profit potential; useful for large caps and recent IPOs
- **Dream Pattern** (cup-with-handle): most reliable; saucer contraction required; William Jiler's original discovery
- **Double Bottom**: "W" shape; undercut preferred to shake out weak holders; cheat/handle on right side
- **Power Play (High Tight Flag)**: stock up ≥100% in ≤8 weeks, then tight flag ≤20% over 3–6 weeks; velocity pattern; no fundamental requirement
- **Buy in order of breakout strength**: let the market tell you which stock to buy first; no favorites

### Section 8 — Position Sizing

| Guideline | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Max equity risk per trade | 1.25–2.5% |
| Max stop-loss | 10% |
| Avg loss target | ≤5–6% |
| Max position size | 50% (exceptional) |
| Optimal position size | 20–25% |
| Number of stocks (small account) | 4–8 |
| Number of stocks (large account) | 10–12 |

- **Kelly Formula / Optimal F**: at 50% PWT and 2:1 reward/risk → optimal position = 25% (4 stocks)
- **Di-worsification**: avoid — too many stocks = average returns, unable to react quickly
- **Two-for-One Rule**: reallocate from two weak performers into one stronger one (sell half each)
- **Portfolio as garden**: pull weeds (losers / dead money), water flowers (leaders)

### Section 9 — When to Sell and Nail Down Profits

- **Base count**: bases 1–2 after correction = best time to buy; bases 3–4 = still workable trading; bases 5–6 = extremely failure prone; sell into late-stage breakouts
- **P/E expansion sell signal**: P/E doubled or more from base 1 entry AND stock in late-stage base = danger
- **Climax top signals** (sell into strength):
  - Stock up 25–50%+ in 1–3 weeks (some 70–80% in 5–10 days)
  - 70%+ up days in 7–15 day window (late-stage only)
  - Largest up day and/or widest daily spread since move began
  - Recent exhaustion gaps
  - Late-stage base 4 or 5
- **Selling into weakness**: largest down day since Stage 2 advance began = almost always outright sell signal
- **Churning**: elevated volume with little price progress = distribution
- **50-day trailing stop (Breakeven or Better Rule)**: once 50-day MA catches up to your breakeven cost, switch stop to 50-day; close below 50-day = exit
- **Free Roll**: when stock is up 2× stop, sell half + move stop to BE → worst case = breakeven on full trade
- **Back Stop**: set at avg gain level or desired minimum profit; not a trailing stop — a fixed floor profit line
- **Sell Half Rule**: indecisive? Sell half; psychological win/win in both directions

### Section 10 — Eight Keys to Superperformance

**For generating big performance:**
1. **Timing** — time the market; buy at the line of least resistance (VCP pivot); velocity trades (20–50% in weeks)
2. **Don't Diversify** — concentrate in 4–12 best names; wide diversification = average returns
3. **Turnover is Not Taboo** — move money to best opportunities; compound edge frequently
4. **Always Maintain Risk/Reward** — re-evaluate every day; never let risk accumulate beyond the potential reward

**For limiting drawdowns:**
1. **Sell into Strength** — sell when buyers are plentiful; compounding multiple 10% returns beats waiting for one 40% return
2. **Trade Small Before You Trade Big** — scale up on winning trades; scale down or stop on losing trades
3. **Always Trade Directionally** — never buy a falling stock; wait for the turn before committing
4. **Protect Your Breakeven Once You Have a Decent Gain** — move stop to BE when stock is up a meaningful amount

### Section 11 — Champion Trader Mindset

- **Mental rehearsal** (not visualization): rehearse both winning and losing scenarios; train response to losses
- **Box breathing**: inhale 4s → hold 4s → exhale 4s → hold 4s; 5 minutes before market open; re-centers focus
- **NLP / cognitive behavioral coaching**: identify cue → routine → reward; replace bad habits with rule-based celebrations
- **Daily review questions**: (1) What went great? (2) What were lessons learned? (3) How will I improve today?
- **Pain/pleasure cycle**: associate pain with large losses, pleasure with small disciplined losses; celebrate correct behavior
- **Three Deadly Trader Traps**: Emotions, Opinions, Ego

## Contradictions with Existing Wiki

| Topic | This Source (Minervini) | Existing Wiki |
|-------|------------------------|---------------|
| **Stop-losses** | MANDATORY; "those who trade without stops eventually stop trading"; 10% max, avg 5–6% | [Connors-MA-Quantified-Approach-2013](Connors-MA-Quantified-Approach-2013.md): stops REMOVE edge from mean-reversion system |
| **Averaging down** | NEVER; "Losers average losers" | [Kalyan-Trading-Philosophy](../analyses/Kalyan-Trading-Philosophy.md): no explicit rule mentioned — neutral |
| **Diversification** | Concentrate in 4–12 best names | [Kalyan-Trading-Philosophy](../analyses/Kalyan-Trading-Philosophy.md): no explicit stance; broadly consistent |
| **Trend direction** | Stage 2 only; never buy Stage 4 | Consistent with all existing strategies |

> **Resolution note**: The stop-loss contradiction between Minervini and Connors is **strategy-specific**. Minervini's SEPA targets momentum/breakout in Stage 2 uptrends; Connors targets mean-reversion dips in existing uptrends. Both can coexist with different rules for each strategy. Do NOT apply Connors no-stop rule to SEPA trades.
