# Book — Prop Trading Secrets: How Successful Traders are Living off the Markets

## Metadata

- **Year:** 2025
- **Format:** epub, 17 chapters (intro + 14 trader interviews + 10-step checklist)

## Key Takeaways

1. **Prop firm funded model**: Pay evaluation fee ($500–$600) → trade a simulated $100K+ account → pass the challenge → get funded → keep 70–90% of profits. Maximum risk to trader = evaluation fee only.

2. **One instrument mastery**: Every successful trader specialises in 1–2 instruments (NQ, ES, EUR/USD repeatedly). Generalism is the enemy of edge.

3. **Risk management > entries**: Nearly every trader independently arrived at risking <1% per trade. "Reduce your position size" is the single most common tip given.

4. **Scale with accounts, not contracts**: Profit by stacking multiple smaller accounts rather than over-leveraging one account. Vince Koehn made $1M this way across 20 Apex accounts.

5. **Resilience is the #1 differentiator**: Traders who made it had 2–7 years of losses and blown accounts before consistent profitability. The ones who stayed used it as tuition.

6. **Prop psychology differs**: Three-strikes rule (stop after 3 losses), first trade sets the daily tone, daily profit goals with trailing-stop protection once hit.

7. **Prop firm evaluation rules differ from funded rules**: In evaluations use daily sub-goals, protect gains. In funded accounts, carry forward the same conservative parameters.

## Detailed Notes

### Prop Trading Model (Chapter 1)
- Prop firms provide simulated capital after a paid evaluation (typical: $500–$600 for $100K challenge)
- Unlike traditional prop: trader doesn't manage client money, no regulatory licensing required
- Trader's max liability = only the evaluation fee; drawdown comes from firm's simulated P&L
- Profit splits: 70–90% to trader
- Key firms mentioned: FTMO, Apex Trader Funding, Topstep, MyForexFunds, Darwinex (fund-level)
- Scale path: small evaluation → track record → stack multiple funded accounts

### 15 Traits of Successful Prop Traders (Chapter 2)
1. Strong work ethic — treat trading as a business
2. First trades set the day's tone — never start with excessive risk
3. Consistency with initial technique — don't strategy-hop
4. Focus on one instrument — depth over breadth
5. Confluence required — never enter on a single signal
6. Cheap entries — enter where stop is tight; asymmetric risk
7. Three-strikes rule — 3 losses → stop trading for the day
8. Lower R:R expectation for day trading — 1:1 fine for high-win-rate scalpers
9. Consistency over large wins — steady cash flow beats home runs
10. Manage expectations — 2–3 years to profitability is normal
11. Adaptability — what worked in 2015 may not work in 2022
12. Self-awareness — track trades honestly; spot your own worst patterns
13. Cut losses fast — held losses become account killers
14. Track all trades — journal is non-negotiable
15. Trading community — peers accelerate growth and provide accountability

### Rob Hoffman — TPT Strategy (Chapter 3)
- Core: wait for strong directional move (Thrust 1), let price pull back, enter on resumption (Thrust 2)
- Works on any instrument, any timeframe
- Entry: thrust closes strongly; pullback shows lower-volume/lesser-range bars; enter on return to direction
- Stop: below pullback low (long) or above pullback high (short)
- See [TPT-Thrust-Pullback-Thrust](../strategies/TPT-Thrust-Pullback-Thrust.md)

### Davide Biocchi — Order Flow / TWbook (Chapter 4)
- Classifies participants: piranhas (retail), white sharks (mid institutions), blue sharks (large institutions/HFT)
- Three DOM order types: spot orders (immediate fill), market orders (sweep the book), hidden/iceberg orders
- TWbook indicator (patented): aggregates and classifies participants in real time
- When "blue sharks" show heavy bid absorption → directional move likely; follow their direction
- VWAP as primary anchor; horizontal volume (Point of Control) as secondary
- See [Order-Flow](../concepts/Order-Flow.md)

### David Floyd — Options Flow + Positive Convexity (Chapter 6)
- 30 years trading S&P 500 futures; 1:1 to 1.2:1 R:R (grinder mentality, very high win rate)
- Tracks options flow as leading indicator for where large players expect price to go
- Manages a "positive convexity" fund: −0.3 correlation to S&P 500; performs well in market downturns
- Philosophy: "I'm just trying to make singles and doubles" — consistency over home runs

### Sunny Harris — SunnyBands + Ultimate-F (Chapter 7)
- 43 years trading; SunnyBands = Dynamic Moving Average (DMA, from her master's thesis on least-squares curve fitting) ± 1.2 ATR inner bands and ± 2.0 ATR outer bands
- 78% win rate, 2:1 R:R on 5-min charts
- Entry: price pulls back to inner SunnyBand in trend direction
- Exit: price reaches outer band on opposite side
- Ultimate-F: modified Ralph Vince optimal-f; reduces deep drawdowns while maintaining compound growth

### Ali Crooks — Market Condition Formula (Chapter 8)
- Starts with 25 markets; applies "market condition formula" → filters to 2–3 active setups at a time
- Three strategies: (1) trend-following with trailing stop, (2) fixed 2:1 R:R, (3) counter-trend with HTF discretionary exit (avg 3.8R)
- Best trade: 13.5:1 over 2 months (built position over two months; required patience to hold)
- Key principle: risk so small it feels "uncomfortable" — that is the correct risk level
- Black swan lesson (Swiss franc unpeg): risked 1%, got slipped to 5.5% loss; still survived; if anyone risked 6–7%, they were wiped out
- Skills: resilience, long-term perspective, self-awareness — listed as top 3 qualities for fund traders

### Aatu Kokkila — Event Trading (Chapter 9)
- Finnish trader, started 2012, $32M AUM; Best Performing Currency Systematic Strategy (Hedge Fund Journal 2022, 2023)
- Strategy: systematic trading of fundamental events (central bank statements, CPI/NFP/PMI data releases)
- Research library: 3,000+ events researched up to 30 years back; only trades ~10–12 events/month
- Average hold: ~3 hours; losers cut in 30 minutes; winners run for days
- Sizing: conviction-based; up to 5:1 leverage on highest-conviction events; most at no leverage
- Ex-WoW top global team: dedication, deliberate practice, team synergy transferred directly to trading
- Key insight: a team is essential for event trading — too much to monitor in real time alone

### Andres Granger — Market-Neutral Arbitrage (Chapter 10)
- Based in South Korea; $10M crypto fund + personal scalping on DAX/NQ
- Primary strategy: cash-futures spread arbitrage (buy spot discount vs. futures → collect convergence)
- Market-neutral: 27% annual return vs. directional fund's 75% in a trending year — less upside, no directional risk
- Directional fund: 100% automated, 5-day/30-day MA crossover trend following
- Tools: Bookmap, QuantTower (order book), Coinglass (crypto: funding rates, open interest, liquidations)
- Tip: "First cut is always the cheapest" — exit losers fast
- Tip: record video of all trades; review at quarter speed to spot decision errors

### Matt Miller — Initial Balance Strategy (Chapter 14)
- NQ scalper; $309,400 from 85 trading days; multiple Apex funded accounts
- Strategy: wait for IB to form (9:30–10:30 a.m. EST); trade breakout of IB high/low; wait for retest; confirm with positive delta; target 10 NQ points
- "Overfunded and under-leveraged": work on the largest account possible relative to contract size
- Three-strikes rule: 3 losses → stop trading for the day, no exceptions
- See [Initial-Balance](../concepts/Initial-Balance.md) and [Prop-Scalp-NQ-Initial-Balance](../strategies/Prop-Scalp-NQ-Initial-Balance.md)

### Nick Syiek — 3-Pillar Scoring System (Chapter 15)
- Computer science background; built Edge Finder tool; 3 years to breakeven, $3M net worth by 23
- Three pillars: (1) Technical (MA crossover + market structure), (2) Sentiment (COT, retail broker positioning, put/call ratios), (3) Fundamental (PMI, CPI/PPI, NFP, interest rate cycles)
- Numerical scoring: each sub-component gets −1/0/+1; aggregated score determines whether to look at charts
- Entry: charts are the final decision point; fundamentals/sentiment filter which charts to examine
- Exit: no fixed take profit; trailing stop behind market structure (trails higher lows in uptrend)
- Avg: 2–3 trades/week; 1–7 day hold; best winner held 6 weeks (Oct–Dec 2023 Nasdaq)
- Key tip: "reduce your position size and see what happens to your trading"
- Correlation management: cap total dollar risk on correlated pairs (e.g., EUR/USD + GBP/USD share USD exposure)

### Vince Koehn — Fibonacci + S/R Multi-TF (Chapter 16)
- Built metal sheet business from $700K to $6M/yr; licensed pilot; now based in US after Asia
- Lost $350K on 20 Apex accounts before making $1M on next 20 — same approach, different discipline
- Strategy: mark Fibonacci on daily/weekly by hand; color-code S/R by timeframe; auto-plot daily S/R on 5-min chart; wait for pullback to confluence zone; entry when next 5-min candle opens and confirms direction
- Value area play (Market Profile): if price re-enters previous day's value area → 80% probability of filling it
- Bookmap: adjusts entries/exits ±8–10 NQ points based on liquidity cluster vs. theoretical level
- Best window: 10 a.m.–11 a.m. EST (IB has formed; avoid the open and close)
- Lightbulb: "scale with accounts, not contracts" — $500/account × 20 accounts = $10K/day; no over-leverage
- Key advice from Apex CEO Darrell Martin: "make $200 and stop; bank it; do it again the next day"

### 10-Step Checklist (Chapter 17)
1. Assess your experience level; learn your chosen instrument deeply
2. Choose instrument: CFDs vs. futures vs. forex (understand spreads, ticks, leverage)
3. Choose style: day trading vs. swing trading based on your schedule
4. Learn the difference: trend-following vs. counter-trend (picking tops/bottoms)
5. Learn strategy from multiple sources (YouTube, live streams, courses); don't rely on one guru
6. Determine trade management: scale out vs. let run; stop-loss philosophy; R:R targets
7. Practice on demo until consistently profitable before paying for a prop challenge
8. Set daily and weekly profit goals; use trade copiers to amplify if hitting them consistently
9. Choose prop firm carefully (longevity, tech, liquidity providers, reputation, reviews, rules)
10. Read ALL terms and conditions; know every rule before trading

**Prop firm due diligence factors:**
- Longevity (>2 years in business)
- In-house technology vs. third-party white label
- Liquidity providers named transparently
- Reputation on Trustpilot + PropFirmMatch
- EAs allowed? News trading allowed? Swing trading allowed (hold over weekend)?
- Customer service quality and response time

## Actionable Insights for Kalyan

1. **One instrument focus**: Apply mastery principle to NSE swings; pick VCP + EMA pullback as primary and don't add more setups until those are mastered
2. **Three-strikes rule**: After 3 losing trades in any session, stop; protects against revenge trading
3. **First trade discipline**: Never open with a full-size position; pilot buy first
4. **Prop firms as capital amplification**: After 6+ months of consistent live profitability, consider using a forex/futures prop firm as an alternative capital path
5. **Daily profit goal protection**: Set a realistic daily target; once hit, trail stops and don't force more trades
6. **Event trading application**: Build a research library of how NSE index/stocks historically react to quarterly results by sector — similar to Aatu's event research model
7. **Overfunded/underleveraged**: When trading live, keep position sizes small relative to account; the buffer helps psychology and prevents ruin
8. **Video review**: Record screen during trading sessions; review at slow speed to identify error patterns
