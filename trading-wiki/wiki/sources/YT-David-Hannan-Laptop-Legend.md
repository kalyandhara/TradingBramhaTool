# YouTube — David Hannan (Laptop Legend): Small Cap Trader, Pyramiding & Parabolic Shorts

## Metadata
- **Source**: YouTube — Titans of Tomorrow podcast
- **URL**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PBhrBnrtbxs
- **Guest**: David Hannan ("Laptop Legend") — small cap trader; $2,000 starter account → $9M+; 158-day no-loss streak; 41% win rate; known for short selling parabolic stocks; lost ~$1M in one hour in October 2022 on a pump-and-dump gone wrong, then rebuilt completely
- **Date ingested**: 2026-06-18

## Key Takeaways

1. **Pyramiding = recycling the same risk** — as a trade moves in your favor, tighten the stop to create room to add at the same dollar-risk level; repeat → same initial $10K risk can become $1M gain
2. **Exponential bet sizing** — not all setups are equal; bet exponentially more on rare A+ setups (8x–20x normal risk) vs standard setups; Pareto confirmed: top 14% of trading days = 82% of annual profits
3. **Mean reversion > breakouts for win rate** — parabolic stocks ALWAYS pull back; win rate on first-red-day short is higher than breakout long because an overextended stock must come back; breakouts can go nowhere
4. **First Red Day setup** — multi-day gap-up extension → blow-off volume + accelerating gaps → first red signal → short entry; works on small caps AND large caps when behaving like small caps (SMCI, MSTR examples)
5. **Trade management = dynamic, not set-and-forget** — if trade reverses to your average price = thesis is already invalidated; move quickly; break-even quickly on big size; trail with 2-min bar lows or 5-min 9 EMA
6. **Broken slot machine philosophy** — find where your edge actually is; small caps are less efficient = more exploitable; don't waste time on markets where the odds aren't tilted your way
7. **Time of day matters** — cleaner entries around open; 1–2pm breakouts have lower follow-through and higher failure rate; exception: large-picture daily setups override
8. **Technicals over fundamentals** — "markets can remain irrational longer than you can remain solvent"; fundamentals players get destroyed fighting parabolic moves; technicals prevent stubborn bad-side positioning
9. **ADR matters for swings** — 7–8% ADR stocks move your portfolio meaningfully; 2% ADR stocks are opportunity cost; focus on high-ADR names for swing trades
10. **Psychology auto-manages via process** — didn't actively work on psychology; desensitization comes from repetition and strict process adherence; largest losses came from deviating from the plan

## Detailed Notes

### Pyramiding — The Core Technique

**Mechanism** (recycling risk):
1. Enter initial position with a defined dollar risk (e.g. $10K)
2. Trade moves in your favor and develops a new structure (higher low on a long, lower high on a short)
3. Tighten stop to the new structure → dollar risk at that tighter stop is now less than $10K
4. Add to position so total dollar risk at the new stop = original $10K
5. Trade moves further → repeat
6. Final position size can be 10–100x initial; same initial risk has been recycled into massive exposure

**Bitcoin example (July)**: started with $10K risk on BitX ETF options; pyramided 4 times on the Bitcoin breakout; final gain = $1M in 4 days. Entry risk was $10K; final position turned that $10K into $1M when the trade ran.

**Mistakes to avoid**:
- Adding aggressively on the breakout through resistance (bad average, wide stop, stops out on first pullback)
- The correct add point: after a "wash and reclaim" — price flushes below a support level (clearing stops), then reclaims; add versus the low of that wash (tight stop, good average)

**Structure for adds**:
- Look for support levels being washed and reclaimed (same as Kalyan's sweep-reverse model)
- Add on reclaim vs. low of wash; do NOT add on the initial breakout through resistance

### Exponential Bet Sizing

**Core principle**: poker analogy. Pocket Aces ≠ Jack-Jack in EV; don't bet the same on both.

**Application in trading**:
- Normal setup: 1x risk
- Good setup: 2–3x risk
- A+ (rare) setup: 8–20x risk
- Exponential, not linear: the jump from A to A+ is not 2x, it is 8x–10x

**The Pareto data point** (David's own 2024 stats):
- Top 14% of trading days → 82% of annual profits
- This is the reason to be in the market daily (stay in flow, build cushion, keep sharp) while saving the big bets for the right moments

**Risk hierarchy** (David's examples):
- Bitcoin breakout setup = lower initial conviction (new pattern for him) → $10K initial risk only
- First red day on MSTR after parabolic = very high conviction → $150K–$200K risk

**Warning**: exponential sizing with mean reversion setups creates possible ironic outcome where the biggest bets come first on the worst-timed events; mitigated by strict entry discipline (only bet big when ALL factors align — see First Red Day checklist)

### Mean Reversion vs. Breakout

| | Breakout | Mean Reversion (First Red Day) |
|--|---------|-------------------------------|
| Win rate | Low (30–40%) | High (60–70%+) |
| R:R | High (if runs) | Moderate–high |
| Worst case | Breaks out, comes back — false breakout | Stock keeps running (rare if all factors align) |
| Why it works | Institutional momentum | Rubber band physics: overextended must snap |
| Best for exponential sizing? | No (lower win rate = painful big losses) | Yes (high win rate = exponential sizing is "safe") |

**Key insight**: David sizes exponentially on mean reversion setups (first red day) precisely BECAUSE the win rate is higher. The math of exponential sizing is most favorable when your win rate is highest.

**Breakout preference**: still trades breakouts (ascending triangles with flat top, increasing slope); prefers tight compression before the break; options + intraday entry timing.

### First Red Day Setup (Parabolic Short)

Full detail in [[strategies/Parabolic-Short-First-Red-Day]].

**Overextension checklist** (all factors for high conviction):
1. Multi-day gap-up extension (3–6+ consecutive up days, each gapping higher)
2. Expanding range: each day's candle bigger than the last
3. Blow-off volume: volume increasing dramatically day by day
4. Gap size accelerating: if prior gaps were $3, new gaps are $8–10
5. 5-min bar off open shows volume dwarfing previous days → euphoria confirmation
6. Extended far above 10-day or 50-day MA
7. Large market cap behaving like small cap (SMCI Feb 2024, MSTR Nov 2024)
8. Low/no intrinsic fundamental value (pre-revenue, narrative-driven, FOMO sector)

**Entry**: short on first break of low-of-day; stop at high-of-day
Alternative: short first 15-min or 30-min candle break after high-of-day

**Why it works**: at peak extension, the rubber band is fully stretched; virtually no new buyers available to sustain; even neutral news triggers profit-taking cascade

**Large cap version**: large caps rarely give this; when they do it's because they've started behaving like small caps (multi-day gap-up, expanding range, FOMO sector). Same setup applies but larger size possible (less slippage).

### Trade Management Philosophy

**Break-even quickly**: if the trade reverses to your entry average (especially in big size), thesis is already invalidated — exit or cut dramatically. Do NOT wait for the original hard stop.

**Why**: largest career loss came from holding a pump-and-dump short that reversed through high-of-day while paralyzed; hundreds of thousands lost. Lesson: when in big size, if it's reverting, GET OUT immediately.

**Two-camp debate**: "set and forget to your stop" vs. David's "dynamic thesis invalidation"
- David's view: when a setup is working, it should NOT be coming back to your average — if it does, the setup is already failing
- Exception: explicitly planned swing trades where intraday chop is acceptable

**Trailing methods**:
- 2-minute bar lows (for clean, fast-moving trades — trail until first higher-high on 2-min)
- 5-min 9 EMA (momentum long trades; exit when 5-min closes below 9 EMA for the first time)
- Daily bar lows (for swing trades; August 5th 2024 Japan yen cascade was perfect example of trailing daily lows on the subsequent reversal)

**Dynamic EV management**: after a straight move down (parabolic short), the stock at -20% on the day no longer has the same EV as when it was up 20%. Lock in profits on straight moves unless it's explicitly a multi-day swing trade.

**Break-even criticism**: David partially rejects "trust your analysis to the hard stop." His counter: thesis can be invalidated before the stop — by reverting to average, by not moving immediately as expected ("tap and go" vs "hanging around")

### Conviction and Sizing

**Not formulaic**: gut feel from experience; not systematized

**General pattern**:
- Very high conviction (first red day on perfect parabolic) → 10–20x normal risk
- Good conviction (breakout setup I know well) → 2–3x normal risk
- Lower conviction (feel trade, early stage) → 1x normal risk

**Why take lower-conviction trades at all?** David's answer:
1. Still positive EV — like finding money on the ground
2. Keeps you in flow with the market
3. Builds P&L cushion heading into the high-conviction moments
4. Removes psychological pressure of waiting for only 2 setups per year

### Broken Slot Machine / Edge Definition

Concept from Lance Brightman: find the "broken slot machine" — the one that's actually paying out, not just the ones taking money. 

Application: small caps are David's broken slot machine. More inefficient, more exploitable, less institutional presence, cleaner patterns (pump-and-dump mechanics, low float dynamics clearly visible). Forex and large caps feel more efficient to him — fewer exploitable moments.

Principle: know exactly where your edge is; define it precisely; stay in those markets/patterns only; resist the urge to trade everything.

### Liquidity and "Rigged" Markets

Low-float stocks (<5M shares): one entity can control the float, paint support levels, sweep stops, then run price in the intended direction. This is why:
1. Slippage risk is enormous (5–10% on stop trigger)
2. The "dip and rip" / sweep-and-reversal pattern is highly visible but dangerous for stops
3. David treats these as lower-conviction setups despite the seductive pattern, because slippage wrecks the R:R

For large liquid stocks: pattern works but needs individual to have enough capital to matter; much cleaner price action without manipulation risk.

## Actionable Insights

1. **Pyramiding**: apply the wash-and-reclaim add technique to Kalyan's sweep-reverse scalp — after a profitable initial sweep-reverse entry, add on the next level's wash-and-reclaim vs the low of that wash
2. **Exponential bet sizing**: when all factors align on a Kalyan A+ setup, consider 3–5x normal risk (not 8–20x as David does — calibrate to Kalyan's experience level with the technique)
3. **Pareto reality check**: track which % of days drive which % of P&L; if Kalyan's numbers show a similar Pareto distribution, the implication is to increase size on the best setups rather than equal-sizing everything
4. **Break-even protocol**: David's approach of "if it returns to average in big size = get out" is consistent with Kalyan's "tap and go" philosophy; validates aggressive break-even moves
5. **Mean reversion setups on Nifty/BankNifty index**: when Nifty or Sensex has 4–6 consecutive up days with expanding range + blow-off volume, the first red day / gap-fill setup becomes a higher-win-rate play than a fresh breakout
6. **ADR filter confirmed**: already in wiki (HB-Stocks stage filter); David independently confirms that 2% ADR vs 7–8% ADR is the difference between portfolios that move and portfolios that don't
