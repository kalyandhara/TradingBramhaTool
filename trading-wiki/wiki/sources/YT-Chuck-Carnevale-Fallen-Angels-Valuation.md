# YouTube — Chuck Carnevale (Fast Graphs): 22 "Fallen Angels"

## Metadata

- **Speaker:** Chuck Carnevale, co-founder of **Fast Graphs** (a valuation-charting product)
- **Type:** Video; supplied by the owner 2026-10-12 as a written summary. **The transcript was not available to this wiki** — everything below is the owner's summary, not checked against the video.
- **Market:** US-listed stocks (and Constellation Software / Thomson Reuters, Canadian). **Nothing here is about India.**
- **Bias:** Cautionary on valuation, not bearish on the businesses.
- **Provenance and weight:** a vendor's own educational content for the product it sells, with **no sample statistics**. Standing weight in this wiki is **zero until measured** (as for the Spider Software cards).

## Key Takeaways

1. **Know intrinsic value before buying any common stock.** Without it you cannot tell whether the price is fair, and overpaying has severe consequences.
2. **A "stealth bear market".** Since 2008–09 the index has been in a long bull market (COVID aside), but many high-quality individual stocks suffered severe corrections after reaching extreme overvaluation.
3. **A great business bought dear is a bad investment.** Stellar operating results and growing earnings do not save a buyer at an inflated multiple: expect volatility, sideways price action, negative annualised returns or years of lost time.
4. **A price chart cannot say whether a stock is "doing well".** Price has to be read against intrinsic value (the Fast Graphs construct).
5. **22 examples** of sharp mean reversion after extreme peaks: Accenture, Adobe, ADP, Align, American Tower, Brown & Brown, Boston Scientific, Blackstone, Constellation Software, EPAM, FactSet, Lululemon, Nike, Paycom, Paychex, PayPal, SBA Communications, Super Micro Computer, Thomson Reuters, Tractor Supply, Xcel Energy, Zoetis.

## Detailed Notes

The method behind the video (from Fast Graphs' public approach, not stated in the owner's summary) draws **price against earnings × a "normal" multiple** over many years, so the gap between the price line and the earnings line shows when a stock was priced above what its earnings supported. The claim is about **multiple compression**: earnings kept growing while price fell, because the multiple fell faster.

## What the video cannot show (read before weighting it)

- **The 22 were chosen because they fell.** A list of fallen stocks proves that some expensive stocks fall; it says nothing about how many expensive stocks did **not**. With no base rate, "overvalued → falls" is untested here.
- **US data, one long bull market, mostly growth/quality names.** The India valuation distribution is different (see below).
- **Intrinsic value is an estimate**, and the video's "normal multiple" is a choice. The result depends on it.
- **The vendor sells the tool** that produces the picture.
