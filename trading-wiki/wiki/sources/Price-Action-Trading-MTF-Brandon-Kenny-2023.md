# Book — The Professional Price Action Trading Book (Kenny Brandon, 2023)

## Metadata
- Author: Kenny Brandon
- Published: 2023 (Kindle Scribe edition)
- File: `raw/Price-Action-Trading-MTF-Brandon-Kenny-2023.pdf` (66 pages, ~15.8 MB)
- Format note: heavily chart/screenshot-based — extracted text is ~48K characters (4 chapters); most pages are annotated chart screenshots with minimal accompanying text, so the book's value is concentrated in the conceptual framework rather than page count

## Key Takeaways

1. **False Break technique** — price breaks a range high/low, traps breakout traders, then reverses back inside the range; their stops add fuel to the reversal move. Fade the false break with a stop beyond the wick.
2. **Buildup** — tight consolidation forming at a level (before or after a breakout); use the buildup's low/high (not the wider range extreme) as a much tighter stop reference. Essentially a discretionary description of the same volatility-contraction pattern that VCP and Darvas Box formalize quantitatively.
3. **Pre-breakout (multi-timeframe) technique** — find a buildup on a higher timeframe (e.g. daily), then drop to a lower timeframe (e.g. 4H) and trade false breaks at the edges of that buildup to enter before the actual breakout fires.
4. **Rubber band snap** — avoid entering in the trend's direction when price is already far from its "area of value" (a respected MA, trendline, or S/R zone); the stop required (placed beyond that area of value) becomes too wide, and price tends to pull back into the area before continuing, stopping out late entries.
5. **Four Stages of the Market** — Accumulation → Advancing → Distribution → Declining, gated by 200-day MA behavior (flat/whipsawed in 1 & 3, sloping in 2 & 4). This is a plain-language restatement of Wyckoff/Weinstein stage analysis already in the wiki ([[concepts/Stage-Analysis]]), without Minervini's 8-criterion precision.
6. **M.A.E Trading Formula** — Market Structure (what stage / direction) → Area of Value (S/R, respected MA, trendline) → Entry trigger (reversal candlestick: Hammer, Shooting Star, Bullish/Bearish Engulfing). A simple 3-step discretionary checklist that sequences existing wiki concepts.
7. **Philosophy** — price action traders enter earlier and exit earlier than indicator-based traders (e.g., MA-crossover traders enter only after the cross, which lags); news rarely overturns an established trend, so trend-following price action implicitly "predicts" the market's reaction to news.

## Detailed Notes

### False Break
- Occurs when price breaks a swing high/low, traps breakout traders on the wrong side, then closes back inside the range.
- Breakout traders' stop-losses (placed near the broken level, mid-range, or near the opposite range extreme) become forced orders in the reversal direction once triggered, accelerating the move.
- Pro tips: (a) the stronger/faster the initial breakout momentum, the better the fade — strong impulsive moves are more likely to be exhausted false moves; (b) avoid "stair-stepping" price action grinding slowly into a level — prior swing highs along the staircase represent overhead selling pressure that caps reward, unlike a single sharp thrust into the level which leaves room to run.

### Buildup
- A buildup is volatility contraction at a level — same phenomenon as VCP's final micro-contraction or a Darvas box, described qualitatively rather than algorithmically.
- The 20-period MA touching the buildup's lows (in an uptrend) or highs (in a downtrend) is used as an informal readiness signal — the MA "catching up" to consolidation suggests the move is imminent.
- Buildups can form before OR after an initial breakout (e.g., price gaps through resistance, then builds up just above it before continuing) — don't assume only one sequence.

### Pre-breakout / Multi-Timeframe Technique
1. Identify a buildup on a higher timeframe (daily).
2. Drop to a lower timeframe (4H) and look for false-break setups at the edges of that same buildup.
3. Enter the false break in the anticipated breakout direction before the higher-timeframe range actually breaks.
4. Manage with partial profit-taking at the buildup's opposite edge (since reaching it doesn't guarantee continuation) — take ~50% off there, let the rest ride, and the position can often be moved to breakeven by that point since some profit is already banked.

### Rubber Band Snap
- "Area of value" = a level the market has repeatedly respected — a tested MA (50, 200), trendline, or trend channel boundary.
- If price is far from that area of value, a stop beyond it (the only technically valid stop) is too wide and kills risk:reward.
- Wait for price to pull back near the area of value before entering with the trend — tighter stop, better R:R, and avoids buying/selling into an extended move that's likely to snap back first.

### Four Stages of the Market
| Stage | Description | 200-day MA behavior |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Accumulation | Range after ≥5-month decline | Flattening; price whips around it |
| 2. Advancing | Higher highs/lows | Sloping up, price above |
| 3. Distribution | Range after ≥5-month advance | Flattening; price whips around it |
| 4. Declining | Lower highs/lows | Sloping down, price below |

Trading implication: only buy in Stage 2 (breakouts/pullbacks), only sell/short in Stage 4; in Stages 1 and 3, trade the range edges (false breaks, buildup breakouts) since direction is undetermined. Multi-timeframe note: the same 4-stage cycle exists at every timeframe — a daily-timeframe distribution stage can appear as a head-and-shoulders top on the 4H chart.

### Support & Resistance
- Support = horizontal zone where buyers are expected to push price up; Resistance = where sellers are expected to push price down.
- S/R roles flip on a break: broken support traps longs at breakeven (forced sellers on retest) and creates regret-driven shorts from traders who missed the breakdown — explaining why broken support becomes resistance and vice versa. (Same three-group psychology already documented in [[concepts/Support-Resistance]].)
- Drawing rules: zoom out to ≥200 bars, only mark the most obvious levels (if you have to guess, skip it), and tune exact placement to maximize "touches" (body or wick).
- Dynamic S/R: in strongly trending markets, horizontal zones stop working — use the 20-period MA, trendlines, or trend channels as moving S/R instead.

### Candlestick Basics (M.A.E. entry trigger)
Covers the four foundational reversal patterns at a basic level (Hammer, Shooting Star, Bullish/Bearish Engulfing) — all already documented in more depth and with statistical validation in [[concepts/Candlestick-Patterns]] from the Litchfield & Morris source. Adds one practical heuristic: read any candle by asking (1) where did price close relative to its range (who's in control), and (2) how big is this candle vs. recent candles (how much conviction is behind the move) — this generalizes pattern recognition without memorizing every named pattern.

### M.A.E Trading Formula
1. **Market Structure** — is price trending up, down, or ranging (= which of the 4 stages)?
2. **Area of Value** — trade only from S/R, a respected MA, or a trendline; don't chase price mid-range.
3. **Entry trigger** — wait for a reversal candle (Hammer/Shooting Star/Engulfing) at the area of value before committing.

### Price Action Philosophy
- Indicators can be reparametrized to support any bias; price itself cannot — it forces objective reads.
- "Predicting" the market, in this framework, means correctly anticipating trend continuation, not picking exact tops/bottoms.
- A single news event rarely overturns an established trend; when news contradicts the prevailing trend, the resulting move is usually just a retracement before the trend resumes — so trend-following price action traders effectively pre-empt the market's reaction to news.
- Price action entries/exits lead indicator-based entries/exits (e.g., MA-crossover signals lag because they require the cross to complete first).

## Actionable Insights

- The **pre-breakout multi-timeframe technique** (HTF buildup + LTF false break) is a concrete, immediately testable entry refinement that could tighten entries on existing VCP/Darvas/breakout setups already in the wiki — worth backtesting against the VCPBot pivot-breakout logic.
