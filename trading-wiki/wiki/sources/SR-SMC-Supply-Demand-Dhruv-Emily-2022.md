# Book — How To Win in The Trading Game Using Support and Resistance, Smart Money Concept, Supply and Demand

## Metadata
- **Authors**: Emily Dhruv
- **Year**: 2022
- **File**: epub via libgen
- **Length**: ~24K chars (short practical guide)

## Key Takeaways

1. **Smart Money = accumulation in sideways, then aggressive price move** — three observable signatures: sideways price action (low volume), aggressive initiation candles, strong rejections (pin bars, two-bar reversals)
2. **Supply and Demand zones are S/R areas** — supply zone = broad resistance; demand zone = broad support; when supply broken it becomes demand (flip zone)
3. **Four S&D formations**: RBR (continuation long), DBD (continuation short), RBD (reversal short), DBR (reversal long)
4. **Zone strength factors**: strength of exit move, time spent in zone (less = stronger), distance price traveled before returning (more = better R:R), freshness (first retest is strongest)
5. **S&R are zones, not lines** — trading to an exact line causes undershoot misses and overshoot false breakout losses; draw a zone between the candle body and wick
6. **S&R psychology**: longs, shorts, and uncommitted all have interest in re-testing the same zone — this is why the zone holds on the first retest
7. **S&R strength factors**: number of touches (first retest = strongest; more touches = weaker), volume at creation, size of exit move, time at level, distance of excursion
8. **Multi-timeframe hierarchy**: higher timeframe S&R has higher probability; mark weekly levels first, trade off daily/hourly
9. **Breakout conditions**: SR breaks into trends (support in downtrend, resistance in uptrend); tight range at SR = imminent break; more frequent tests = weaker level; higher lows into resistance → ascending triangle → breakout
10. **Odds enhancers**: trend alignment, sector/index alignment, dominant pressure confirmation

## Detailed Notes

### Smart Money Activity — Three Signatures

**1. Sideways Price Action (Low Volume)**
Smart Money builds positions slowly in a consolidation area to avoid moving price against itself. These areas are very important regardless of timeframe. Low volume confirms that the accumulation is not yet visible to the market.

**2. Aggressive Initiation Activity**
After accumulation is complete, Smart Money executes aggressively — large candles moving quickly in one direction. This is the move away from the base that identifies the S&D zone. The further and faster the move, the stronger the zone.

**3. Strong Rejection**
Sudden price reversal at a level — pin bars, two-bar reversals (engulfing). Indicates that strong participants aggressively rejected a price area. The location of the rejection becomes a S/R zone to be defended on the next approach.

**Odds enhancers (three-part checklist)**:
1. Trading with the trend
2. Trading from S/R or S&D zone
3. Trading with dominant pressure (buying pressure in demand zone, selling pressure in supply zone)

### Market Structure
Accumulation → Trend Up (Rally) → Distribution → Trend Down (Drop)
- Reaccumulation: smaller consolidation within uptrend; Redistribution: smaller consolidation within downtrend

### Four S&D Formations

| Name | Pattern | Trade Direction |
|------|---------|-----------------|
| Rally Base Rally (RBR) | Rally → consolidation → rally resumes | Long from base on retest (continuation) |
| Down Base Down (DBD) | Drop → consolidation → drop resumes | Short from base on retest (continuation) |
| Rally Base Drop (RBD) | Rally → consolidation → drop | Short from base on retest (reversal = supply zone) |
| Down Base Rally (DBR) | Drop → consolidation → rally | Long from base on retest (reversal = demand zone) |

**How to identify**: scan chart for series of large consecutive candles → identify the base (sideways area) from which those candles originated → the base is the zone.

### Zone Strength (Rank Order)
1. **Freshness** — first retest of a zone is the strongest entry. Each subsequent test weakens the zone (orders are being filled and consumed).
2. **Exit move strength** — the bigger and faster the move away from the zone, the larger the order imbalance. A huge candle away = strong zone.
3. **Time at zone** — less time in the base = more aggressive Smart Money entry = stronger zone. A very brief consolidation before a large move = very strong zone.
4. **Distance before return** — the further price traveled before returning, the higher the R:R and the higher the probability it holds (buyers below the demand zone have confirmed their absence, sellers above the supply zone likewise).
5. **Volume at zone** — higher volume at zone creation = more market interest = higher probability of defense.

### When Zones Break
- Price spending too long near a zone (zone tested and retested) → orders consumed → zone weakens → breaks
- Strong move into the zone overwhelms remaining orders
- A **low-volume test** of a zone confirms it is likely to hold (confirms remaining orders are intact)
- If price stays close to zone without rejecting → bears/bulls unable to push → likely break in opposite direction

### Entry Process (Multi-Timeframe)
1. HTF (Higher Timeframe): locate S&D zone / S&R level
2. TTF (Trading Timeframe): wait for price to reach the zone; look for acceptance or rejection
3. Entry signals on TTF: pin bar, engulfing, outside candle
4. Only enter in direction of dominant trend
5. Day trading S&D: prior day's high and low are S&D zones for the next session

### S&R Psychology (Why Zones Work)

Three participant groups at any S&R zone:
- **Longs**: bought near support; if price returns, they add to a winning position
- **Shorts**: in pain; hoping price returns to their entry to break even (will close → buy pressure = more demand)
- **Uncommitted**: missed the move; waiting to enter long on next dip

All three groups create demand at the same level → zone self-fulfills on first retest.

When support breaks: all three groups were wrong; the support zone now becomes resistance (longs now sellers at breakeven, shorts vindicated, uncommitted now sellers).

### Drawing S&R Zones (Two-Step Method)
1. Switch to **line chart** (closes only); identify swing high/low rejection points; mark the level
2. Switch back to **candlestick chart**; draw a zone from the candle **body** to the candle **wick** around that line

Use zones, not lines, because:
- **Undershoot**: price approaches your line but doesn't quite reach it → missed trade → zone would have caught it
- **Overshoot (Upthrush / Spring)**: price briefly pierces your line then reverses → false breakout → zone accommodates the spike

### Types of S&R
1. **Horizontal**: swing highs and lows, pivot points, congestion areas, prior rejections
2. **Dynamic**: moving averages (most effective in trending markets)
3. **Fibonacci retracements**: 23.6%, 38.2%, 50%, 61.8%, 100% of major swings — most common S&R projection method

### S&R Flip Zones
- Broken resistance → becomes support (on pullback, go long)
- Broken support → becomes resistance (on bounce, go short)
- "Flipping" happens because the supply/demand balance at that level has inverted

### Multi-Timeframe S&R
- Higher TF has higher probability
- Workflow: mark weekly levels → plot on daily → look for TTF entries
- Prevents chart clutter; keeps focus on highest-probability levels only
- Only mark **valid levels** (major swing points, congestion zones, prior rejection areas); ignore every minor retracement

### Breakout Conditions
- Support breaks in downtrends; resistance breaks in uptrends
- Tight consolidation at S&R = imminent break (no pressure from either side)
- Higher lows into resistance → ascending triangle → resistance break
- Lower highs into support → descending triangle → support break
- More frequent tests of a level = more orders consumed = weakening = likely break

### Day Trading S&R
- Prior day's high = resistance / supply zone for next session
- Prior day's low = support / demand zone for next session
- Validate with TTF price action signals (pin bar, engulfing)

## Actionable Insights

1. **Use zone width, not exact lines** for all S&R entries — eliminates undershoot misses and false-breakout exits
2. **Always mark from HTF down** — weekly → daily → 1H before trading; only trade at levels visible on the higher TF
3. **First retest = best entry** — freshness is the #1 zone strength factor; avoid 3rd+ retests
4. **Day trading default**: prior day H/L as S&D zones; validate with entry signal candle on trading TF
5. **Ascend/descend triangle breakouts**: higher lows into resistance (ascending) → buy the breakout; lower highs into support (descending) → sell the breakdown
6. **Flip zones**: when a clear support breaks, wait for price to return and test it from below (now resistance) → short entry; vice versa for broken resistance
